CII OBLITERATED — UNIDENTIFIED "FORCE DROIDS" ANNIHILATE DISSIDENT FACTION IN SINGLE ENGAGEMENT
◆ BBY 22 — 06:14:02 GST ◆ CORUSCANT BUREAU ◆ CORRESPONDENT: FINIS VALORUM JR.

CORUSCANT — In what Republic Intelligence is describing as one of the most sudden and complete military annihilations in the history of the Clone Wars, the Confederación de la Igualdad e Independencia — known across the Outer Rim and among dissident circles under the initials CII — has been utterly destroyed. The organization, which had been growing in size and influence for several standard years, was wiped out in a single engagement of remarkable brevity against a series of unidentified droid units displaying capabilities that senior Republic analysts and at least two members of the Jedi Council are struggling, by all accounts, to classify. The working designation applied to the attackers in classified Senate briefings — already leaking into intelligence circles — is "Force Droids." No such designation has any known precedent in the military doctrine of either the Galactic Republic or the Confederacy of Independent Systems.

The CII was not a conventional rebel faction. It was founded in the shadow of the Clone Wars by individuals who had grown disillusioned with both sides of the conflict, and its composition reflected that breadth of rejection. Its membership included defectors from CIS command — officers who had seen the inner workings of the Separatist machine and found it no less corrupt than the institution it claimed to oppose — alongside soldiers recruited from dozens of worlds throughout the Mid and Outer Rim. They wore no colors of either the Republic or the CIS. Instead, members were distinguished by a striking and deliberate symbol: a yellow hexagon emblazoned on their armor, representing neither side's geometry, neither side's claim to legitimacy. A symbol of refusal. The faction's total membership count at the time of its destruction is estimated by intelligence analysts at somewhere between three and six thousand combatants, supported by an unknown number of civilian sympathizers on at least fourteen worlds.

What made the CII philosophically distinct — and what made it so difficult for the Republic to categorize — was its code of conduct. The faction did not wage war the way either belligerent in the Clone Wars did. Their doctrine was explicit and, by multiple accounts, genuinely observed: enemies were to be incapacitated, not killed. Lethal force was authorized only when an adversary was deemed an irreducible threat to the lives of CII personnel or the continuation of the faction's mission. Those captured in battle were not imprisoned in field camps or transferred to labor facilities. They were transported — regardless of how far that required traveling — to CII judicial councils, where a formal legal proceeding, with representation and evidence, determined their fate. Republic officers who had been through this process and subsequently released described it, privately and with evident discomfort, as "more fair than anything the Senate has produced in a decade."

The faction's ideological foundation drew comparisons to pre-Republic governmental philosophies and to certain strains of contemporary political thought that have gained traction on worlds dissatisfied with the Senate's conduct of the war. In broad terms, the CII believed that the Galactic Republic had become a vehicle for concentrated power rather than distributed justice — that the Senate, whatever its origins, had evolved into an instrument of the interests of its most powerful members, not its most numerous citizens. They also believed — and this element is perhaps the most explosive — that the CIS had a secret agenda that went beyond the stated aims of the Separatist movement. The precise nature of that alleged agenda has not been made public, and whether the CII possessed specific intelligence on it died with the faction. The Senate has declined to comment on whether any documents recovered from CII strongholds are being examined.

The organization's founder, supreme commander, and the face of its ideology was a human man known publicly as Halgane Litole — referred to by his followers almost exclusively simply as Litole. His background before the founding of the CII is partially documented: he held a mid-level command position within the CIS before his defection, which appears to have been precipitated by direct exposure to the upper echelons of Separatist leadership. Those who knew him described a patient and meticulous strategist, a man who planned in years rather than months, and who held a genuine and deeply felt conviction that the galaxy's two warring powers were not opposites but mirrors. His current status is UNKNOWN. No confirmed identification of his remains has been produced. Republic search teams operating at the site of the final engagement have found no trace of him among the dead.

The engagement itself lasted, by all accounts, less than four hours. The site — a fortified facility on the edge of the Outer Rim, whose location this correspondent has agreed not to publish pending ongoing security operations — was reduced to structural ruin by energies that Republic forensic teams cannot match to any catalogued weapon system in existence. Witnesses in the surrounding area, a small number of non-combatant civilians, described the attackers in terms that have been consistent across independent interviews. The droids moved, they said, with a quality of awareness that standard battle droids do not possess. They seemed to anticipate movement before it occurred. Blaster fire was deflected — not by shields, but by physical repositioning executed at inhuman speed and precision. One witness, speaking on condition of anonymity, offered the statement that has since circulated in intelligence circles: "They weren't fighting like droids. They were fighting like something that knew what was going to happen before it did."

The Jedi Council has been formally notified. No public statement has been issued. Senate hearings on the matter are expected to be convened within the week, though whether those hearings will be classified remains under discussion. This correspondent urges all citizens to treat unverified reports with appropriate caution while acknowledging that the destruction of the CII, whatever one thought of its politics, represents an event of significant consequence — and that the weapon used to achieve it remains entirely unaccounted for.

ANCIENT FURY REBORN — UNIDENTIFIED GEONOSIAN WARRIOR FACTION STRIKES OUTER RIM IN COORDINATED CAMPAIGN OF UNPRECEDENTED FEROCITY
◆ BBY 22 — 08:47:33 GST ◆ OUTER RIM BUREAU — FIELD TRANSMISSION ◆ CORRESPONDENT: DENNII SUNBER

OUTER RIM TERRITORIES — I am filing this report from a secure relay station three systems removed from the most recent engagement site, and I want the editors and the citizens reading this to understand something before the facts begin: what is happening in the Outer Rim right now does not fit any existing category of threat that this correspondent — or, by the accounts reaching me, any Republic military analyst — has encountered during the Clone Wars. A faction has emerged from Geonosis that is not the Separatists, not an extension of Poggle the Lesser's apparatus, and not a criminal organization operating under a political banner. It is something older than all of those things, and considerably more dangerous.

Clone commanders across at least seven separate engagement sites have used the same phrase in their after-action reports, independently and without apparent coordination: "warrior caste." The attackers are Geonosian — insectoid, some winged, physically formidable in a way that the drone-caste Geonosians encountered throughout the Clone Wars simply are not. They conduct ground assaults personally and deliberately. They close distance. They fight in melee range. They use their own bodies as primary weapons and treat the destruction of an enemy as an act requiring personal presence. Republic troopers — veterans of Christophsis, of Ryloth, of a dozen campaigns across three years of war — have described these warriors as the most physically dangerous adversaries they have encountered in the field. That assessment, from soldiers who have faced Jedi, Sith assassins, and the full weight of Separatist droid armies, is not a statement this correspondent makes lightly.

Droids are present in this faction's operations, but their role is categorically secondary. The faction fields its own fleet — vessels of Geonosian design but not matching any configuration in the Republic's recognition databases — and deploys automated units for perimeter enforcement, fire support, and logistical functions. But in every ground engagement documented thus far, droids have served as tools. Scaffolding. The killing, as every survivor account confirms, is done by the warriors. This is not incidental. It is, by all evidence, doctrine.

To understand why requires understanding the grievance at the movement's core, and that grievance is directed at a specific individual: Poggle the Lesser, Archduke of Geonosis, the Geonosian leader who forged the planet's alliance with Count Dooku and the CIS in the years before the Battle of Geonosis. The terms of that alliance are well known: Geonosis became the primary manufacturing base for the Separatist droid army. Its tunnels, its foundries, its ancient arenas, the physical substance of Geonosian civilization — all of it was converted, methodically and at an accelerating pace, into production infrastructure for machines. Poggle delivered his world to the war. He delivered his people's culture, their history, and their identity to the war. And from the perspective of this emerging faction, he delivered all of it not for Geonosian independence or Geonosian glory, but for the strategic calculations of offworld commanders who view the planet as a manufacturing asset, nothing more.

The warriors will not forgive this. Their attacks on Separatist supply lines have been as aggressive as their attacks on Republic forces — perhaps more so. They do not distinguish between the two powers of the Clone Wars, except insofar as both represent the political order they have rejected. Their stated purpose, communicated openly to at least one Republic officer who survived a brief capture and was released with what appears to have been deliberate care, is to end the Clone Wars — not through diplomacy, not through political pressure, but by dismantling both sides until neither can continue. Beyond that, they intend to rule the galaxy. And they intend to retake Geonosis. These are not the stated ambitions of a resistance movement. These are the stated ambitions of a power that considers itself the legitimate inheritor of galactic authority, waiting for the current occupants to exhaust themselves into irrelevance.

At the center of this faction is a queen. Geonosian social structure has always been organized around queens — biological and political authorities of absolute status within their hive. This faction's queen is referred to in intercepted Geonosian transmissions by a name that Republic xenolinguistics specialists have rendered as Yamilei. What is known of her is limited. She has not been confirmed present at any engagement. Her communications with her forces use encryption methods that have resisted all current Republic decryption efforts. The officer who survived capture described being brought before a presence he never directly saw, in a chamber he could not precisely locate, and receiving a message delivered through intermediaries: the Republic should consider what it has done to worlds like Geonosis, because it will be held accountable. He was then returned to his unit, unharmed.

The faction's tactical discipline is extraordinary. They do not overextend. They do not hold ground they cannot defend. They strike in concentrated bursts, cause maximum damage in minimum time, and withdraw before reinforcements can arrive. Every engagement has ended with the warriors gone. No prisoners have been taken from their side. No equipment has been recovered intact. The Republic's forensic picture of this faction has been assembled almost entirely from what they chose to leave behind, which appears to be exactly nothing of intelligence value. They are learning faster than our analysts can track them.

Glory, as one fragment of recovered Geonosian writing has been tentatively translated to mean, is not measured by what you control. It is measured by what you defeat. By whom you face in combat, directly, with your own strength, and overcome. The old way. The way that Poggle abandoned when he handed Geonosis to Count Dooku. The way that Queen Yamilei, it appears, intends to restore — whether the galaxy consents to that restoration or not. This correspondent will continue to file from the field. Citizens of the Republic are advised to exercise caution in Outer Rim sectors flagged in the accompanying security bulletin. This story is not over. It is, by all indications, just beginning.
